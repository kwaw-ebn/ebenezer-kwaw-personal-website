Building MineGuard Ghana for Earlier Risk Visibility and Accountable Action

By Ebenezer Kwaw · 25 September 2026 · Working MVP
Explore the MVP ↗View GitHub Repository ↗

Mining safety and environmental management depend on timely field reporting, clear responsibility and visible follow through. MineGuard Ghana is a working portfolio MVP that explores how these activities can be connected in one practical decision support system.

I developed MineGuard Ghana to demonstrate a digital workflow for recording incidents and near misses, monitoring environmental observations, assigning corrective actions, reviewing spatial signals and producing management summaries. The project is Ghana focused, but it is not presented as an approved regulatory system.

The central idea

Turn disconnected field observations into an accountable sequence: capture the report, assess its priority, assign action, monitor progress, review location patterns and communicate what requires attention.

One reporting workflowSafety incidents, near misses, community concerns and environmental observations.
Explainable prioritiesVisible reasons behind Routine, Review and Priority signals.
Action accountabilityOwnership, due dates, status tracking and management summaries.

The problem MineGuard addresses

Operational information can lose value when it is spread across paper forms, messages, spreadsheets and separate departmental records. A safety report may be captured without a visible action trail. An environmental concern may be known locally but difficult to compare with observations from other locations. Managers may receive totals without seeing where delays, repeated signals or unresolved actions are accumulating.

Ghana's Minerals Commission publishes the Minerals and Mining Health, Safety and Technical Regulations, 2012, L.I. 2182, and describes inspection responsibilities that include compliance monitoring, accident and incident investigation, pollution investigation and recommendations to prevent recurrence. MineGuard does not replace those legal processes. It demonstrates how structured digital records could support internal visibility and review.

The solution

The MVP connects six operational areas: an overview dashboard, a structured report form, environmental monitoring, cases and corrective actions, an aggregated risk map and downloadable reports. An access preview also demonstrates how future role permissions could separate responsibilities.

What has been built

Risk map and spatial responsibility

The risk map was designed carefully. It does not label a location as illegal, unsafe or noncompliant. It plots only reports submitted into the system with coordinates and groups them into operational review signals. The page explicitly states that locations are demonstration monitoring areas, not evidence of unlawful activity.

When no coordinate enabled reports exist, the map displays an honest empty state instead of synthetic points. This protects the credibility of the demonstration and makes the data origin clear.

Explainable decision support

MineGuard does not claim to predict accidents, determine legal responsibility or automate enforcement. The present MVP applies transparent rules to help reviewers organize attention. The source record remains visible, and a qualified person must interpret the evidence and decide the response.

RoutineNo configured priority condition is present. The report remains available for normal review.
ReviewOne or more indicators require closer assessment or follow up.
PriorityThe submitted information suggests urgent professional review, not an automatic legal conclusion.

Proposed role based access

The Access preview currently demonstrates eight future roles: Administrator, Occupational Health Officer, Mine Safety Officer, Environmental Officer, Community Relations Officer, Management Viewer, Data Analyst and Regulatory Observer.

This is a presentation layer, not completed authentication. A production system would require secure sign in, least privilege permissions, organization separation, audit logs, account approval, session controls and periodic access review before real operational data could be used.

Technical architecture

Frontend
Responsive HTML, CSS and JavaScript interface deployed on Render.
Backend
FastAPI service providing validation, application logic and REST endpoints.
Data layer
SQL based storage with a clear path to managed PostgreSQL for persistent production use.

The frontend sends structured records to the backend API. The service validates each request, stores the report and returns shared records to the dashboard, action tracker, map and reporting views. The application contains no preloaded synthetic incident records.

Privacy, security and governance

The current MVP should be used only with demonstration or properly deidentified information. It is not ready for worker health records, confidential company investigations, regulatory evidence or identifiable community complaints.

Before a controlled pilot
The project requires an approved data model, privacy impact assessment, threat modelling, managed persistent storage, encrypted transport and backups, access enforcement, complete audit logging, retention rules, incident response procedures and formal legal and operational review.

Why the project matters

The value of MineGuard is not a colourful dashboard. Its value proposition is a traceable line from observation to responsibility. A field report becomes useful when the right person sees it, understands why it matters, records an action and can later demonstrate whether the action was completed.

The ILO Safety and Health in Mines Convention emphasizes mine safety and health governance, while its code of practice for opencast mines includes risk assessment, occupational safety management systems and emergency response principles. WHO guidance also highlights the need for public health strategies around artisanal and small scale gold mining and mercury related risks. These sources reinforce the need for evidence, professional oversight and coordinated systems.

Current MVP boundaries

  • No automatic enforcement decision or legal classification
  • No diagnosis or individual worker health prediction
  • No claim that a mapped point proves illegal activity
  • No live connection to government, mining company or laboratory systems
  • No completed production authentication or organization isolation
  • No validated predictive model
  • No authorization for real confidential operational data

Development roadmap

  1. Expert review: validate reporting fields, priority logic, corrective action pathways and management indicators with safety, environmental, community and mining professionals.
  2. Secure accounts: implement authentication, enforced permissions, organization boundaries, audit trails and administrator approval.
  3. Persistent data: connect managed PostgreSQL, backups, recovery controls and data retention policies.
  4. Operational configuration: allow approved organizations to configure sites, areas, report types, action owners and escalation thresholds.
  5. Evidence attachments: add secure photo and document upload with consent, metadata controls and retention rules.
  6. Offline field use: support safe offline capture and controlled synchronization in areas with limited connectivity.
  7. Interoperability: design reviewed exchange pathways for approved organizational and regulatory systems.
  8. Evaluation: test usability, completeness, response time, action closure and user trust through a controlled pilot.
